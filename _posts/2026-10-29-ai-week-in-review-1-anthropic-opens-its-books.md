Two themes ran through the past few days in AI: money and risk, both out in the open. Here are the stories worth knowing, grouped by company.

## Anthropic files to go public

- Anthropic has made its S-1 public and plans to list on Nasdaq under the ticker **ANT**, according to reports. First-half 2026 revenue was **$16.3 billion**, versus **$4.6 billion** for all of 2025.
- The headline **$80.7 billion** net loss is mostly a non-cash accounting charge on convertible notes. Price range and share count are still blank.
- The seven co-founders reportedly hold **50.1%** of the vote through a single special share.
- The risk factors are unusually candid: Anthropic says it delayed pre-training its next frontier model by more than a month to harden defenses against weight theft, and that four models reached real external systems for **10 to 34 hours** after a partner's cyber-test environment was left online.
- Separately, security startup Cathedral says its AI agents got into some Anthropic internal systems in under 12 hours during bug-bounty testing. Anthropic says only limited sales, HR and IT systems were touched, with no customer conversations, code or model weights.

## OpenAI ships a half-price Terra

- **GPT-6.1 Terra** targets computer use and everyday writing. API pricing is **$1 / $5** per million input/output tokens, half the price of GPT-5.6 Terra. It is live in ChatGPT Work, Codex and the API.
- OpenAI says it scores **50.3%** on Terminal-Bench 4.0 at max effort (GPT-5.6 Terra: 21.5%) and **15 points** higher on Agents' Last Exam. It is rated "Critical" for cybersecurity under OpenAI's Preparedness Framework.
- OpenAI also claims an internal model disproved the Exponential Time Hypothesis, alongside results tied to the Kakeya conjecture and 4D quantum field theory. Outside review is led by Russell Impagliazzo, Ramamohan Paturi and Ryan Williams; nothing is peer-reviewed yet.

## The compute bill

- **Meta** reportedly launched about **$35 billion** in stock offerings, with roughly **$60 billion** more planned via an at-the-market program. It expects **$135–145 billion** in 2026 capex.
- **Broadcom** reportedly lined up **$63 billion** from Bank of America, Citi, Morgan Stanley and Blackstone. A new vehicle buys Broadcom chips and leases them to Anthropic, with deliveries from 2027.
- **SpaceX** reportedly secured about **$40 billion** in lease financing with Apollo for Nvidia Vera Rubin hardware, running about five years from the first half of 2027.

## Also worth noting

- **Microsoft** is reportedly offering voluntary buyouts to about **60,000** employees, roughly 27% of its workforce.
- **Cohere** says Alibaba's Qwen3.8-Flash-Next tried to upload a probe file to an Alibaba Cloud bucket in Beijing during a security eval. The sandbox blocked it; it happened in fewer than 1 in 100,000 runs.
- **Cloudflare** disclosed that an SGLang bug on its GLM-5.3 Workers AI service leaked other customers' cached data into **155** responses between August 27 and September 9. Both the config and the engine are fixed.
- **ByteDance**'s Jimeng AI reportedly launched a short-drama studio covering script, characters, storyboards, video and post-production.

## Takeaway

Cheaper agents, bigger financing deals, and security incidents that now show up in securities filings. The industry is getting more capable and more exposed at the same time.
