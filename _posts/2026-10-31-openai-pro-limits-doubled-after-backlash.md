On October 29, OpenAI reversed one of its least popular decisions of the year. Instead of cutting usage on the $200 ChatGPT Pro plan, it doubled limits across all Pro tiers and Business Premium. Here's what changed, based on OpenAI's help center and its staff's public posts.

## How we got here

- **September 29:** OpenAI reopened new Pro 200 sign-ups and emailed existing subscribers that their Work and Codex allowance would be reduced, at the same $200 price. According to ITmedia, Tibo (Thibault Sottiaux, the OpenAI product lead behind ChatGPT and Codex) said that in API-price terms the limit would be half of what it was, while arguing more efficient models meant you could still get more done.
- **October 5:** Tibo started a 28-day run of daily improvements, promising to reset usage limits on any day the team didn't ship a clear one.
- **October 29 (day 25):** "We are doubling all usage limits for all Pro 100/200/500 tiers and Business Premium, effective today." He added that the Pro 200 reduction wouldn't happen.

## What each plan gets now

| Plan | Price / month | Work and Codex allowance | Weekly GPT-6 Pro chat messages |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pro 100 | $100 | doubled | unchanged |
| Pro 200 | $200 | reduction canceled; lower allowance doubled to match the higher one | lower limit raised to match the higher one |
| Pro 500 | $500 | doubled (Ultrafast models included) | unchanged |
| Plus | unchanged | unchanged | unchanged |

Existing subscribers get the new allowance automatically, without waiting for renewal. Dots, OpenAI's always-on background agents, keep their separate extended limits "for now."

## The fine print

OpenAI still hasn't published exact numerical limits for any tier. Its own help page also says a larger allowance "does not guarantee twice as many completed tasks," because consumption depends on the model, task and settings. So "doubled" is a doubling of a number you can't see.

## Why it matters

This is the second time in a few weeks that the price of heavy AI coding usage moved because users complained loudly in public. For developers who live in Codex, the practical effect is real: more agent runs before you hit a wall. But the episode also shows how soft these allowances are. A limit that can be halved by email and doubled by tweet is not something to plan a business around.

If you're choosing between a subscription and paying per token through the API, this is a good argument for keeping your workflow portable.
