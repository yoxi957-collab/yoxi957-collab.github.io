Seedream 5.5 for Storyboards: Preparing Keyframes Before Video

TLDR: How to use Seedream 5.5 to build a storyboard and a set of consistent keyframes before any footage or animation is made, with a shot list template and a continuity checklist.

Film and TV Usability More Than 2x: Using Seedream 5.5 for Storyboard Frames

Storyboards are where many video projects either save time or lose it. A clear set of frames lets a director, an editor, and a client agree on framing, mood, and sequence before anyone films or animates a second of footage. Seedream 5.5 is relevant here because its announcement reports a large gain in exactly this category.

ByteDance Seed introduced Seedream 5.5 on November 9, 2026. In its six-use-case evaluation on the MagicArena platform, the team reports that usability rates in film and television were more than twice those of Seedream 5.0 Pro. That is a vendor figure, but it points to where the model was tuned.

This guide covers still frames only: the storyboard panels and the keyframes that later guide motion work. The workflow is editorial guidance based on the public announcement, not a test report.

Key Takeaways

  • Shot list first: Write every shot with framing, action, and light before generating a single frame.
  • Fix the anchors: Characters, key props, and the main location should come from one approved reference set.
  • Continuity is the main risk: Wardrobe, props, light direction, and screen direction must match from frame to frame.
  • Reference-based series are documented: The announcement shows one set of character assets reused across different settings while keeping defining features.
  • Editing beats regenerating for small fixes: The announcement describes pixel-level preservation of unedited regions during multi-turn editing.
  • Specs still missing: Maximum resolution and reference-image limits for Seedream 5.5 are not yet public.

Step 1: Write the Shot List

A shot list turns a script into a sequence of frames. Each line names the shot number, the framing (wide, medium, close), the action, and the light. Keep one purpose per shot. If a line needs two sentences to describe what happens, it is probably two shots.

Shot list template

#   FRAMING   ACTION                         LIGHT / TIME
01  wide      courier enters rainy street    dusk, wet reflections
02  medium    courier checks parcel label    shop sign glow, left
03  close     label text, readable           same glow, left
04  wide      courier crosses to door        dusk, rain heavier

Light direction belongs in the list because it is the easiest continuity detail to lose. In the example, the glow stays on the left across shots 02 and 03.

Step 2: Build the Anchor Set

Before making panels, generate and approve the frames that everything else depends on: the main character in a neutral pose, any prop that appears in more than one shot, and the main location. These become reference images for the rest of the board.

The announcement describes a designer using the same set of character assets as references to create a series of images in different settings, with the model retaining the characters' defining features and textures while poses, groupings, and composition change. That is the behavior a storyboard needs. Workspaces such as Seedream 5.5 let a creator upload a reference image and state both the intended change and the elements to retain, which maps directly to "same courier, new shot".

Step 3 and 4: Panels, Then Keyframes

Panels can be rough. Their job is to show sequence and framing. Choose one aspect ratio for the whole board, matching the delivery format, so that framing decisions carry over.

Keyframes are the polished frames for shots that matter most, often the first and last frame of a key move. When a keyframe needs a small fix, such as a prop in the wrong hand, editing the existing frame is usually safer than regenerating it, because regeneration can change details that were already approved. The announcement says Seedream 5.5 was trained to preserve unedited regions at the pixel level and to avoid color shifts in those areas.

Step 5: Hand Off

Name files with the shot number from the list, and add a short note for each: what is fixed, what is placeholder, and any detail that must change in final production. Text visible in frames, such as the parcel label in shot 03, should be proofread. The vendor reports a severe text corruption rate of 0.4% and a minor rate of 1.7% in its Chinese and English evaluation, which is low but not zero.

Limitations

The film and television figure comes from the vendor's own evaluation. No independent storyboard or film-production test of Seedream 5.5 had been published at the time of writing.

Maximum resolution, generation speed, and the number of reference images Seedream 5.5 accepts at once are not yet public. Workspaces that host Seedream models state that reference limits and output settings depend on the model selected. Character consistency across many frames should still be checked by eye, frame by frame.

Which Frames to Generate and Which to Draw

The deciding factor is how much a frame must match an exact physical setup.

Generate when

  • Exploring mood: Early boards where light, color, and atmosphere are still open.
  • Client presentations: Polished keyframes that help a non-specialist picture the result.
  • Location concepts: Settings that do not exist yet or are hard to scout.

Draw or photograph when

  • Exact blocking matters: Stunts, choreography, or camera moves with precise positions.
  • A real location is booked: Reference photos of the actual space are more reliable.
  • Product or brand details appear: Items that must match final props exactly.

FAQ

Can Seedream 5.5 keep a character consistent across a storyboard?

The announcement shows a set of character assets reused as references across different settings while defining features are retained. Consistency still needs a frame-by-frame check.

What is the difference between a panel and a keyframe?

A panel shows framing and sequence and can be rough. A keyframe is a polished frame, often the start or end of a key move, used to guide motion work.

Should a storyboard use one aspect ratio?

Yes. Using the delivery aspect ratio for every frame keeps framing decisions consistent from board to final footage.

How should small mistakes in a frame be fixed?

Editing the existing frame is usually safer than regenerating it, because approved details are less likely to change. The announcement describes pixel-level preservation of unedited regions.

Why does the film and TV figure matter?

The vendor reports that usability rates in film and television were more than twice those of Seedream 5.0 Pro, which suggests the model was tuned for this kind of work.

What is still unknown?

Maximum resolution, generation speed, and the reference-image limit for Seedream 5.5 are not yet public.
